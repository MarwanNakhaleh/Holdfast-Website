How it works

Three parts, one job

  1. 01 · DNS FILTER

    Every ad and tracker on the internet has an address, and your devices ask for those addresses thousands of times a day — the brick simply answers "no" to the bad ones.

  2. 02 · ENCRYPTED LOOKUPS

    The lookups it does allow are encrypted with DNS-over-TLS, so your internet provider can't read or sell the list of sites your home visits.

  3. 03 · THE APP

    The iOS companion app finds the brick on your WiFi by itself, pairs with a 6-digit code, and walks you through protecting the whole network.

What to expect

What it kills — and the one thing it can't

Blocked at the source

  • Web ads across every browser in the house
  • In-app banner ads on phones and tablets
  • Trackers and telemetry phoning home
  • Smart-TV home-screen ads

The honest limitation

In-stream video ads on streaming services — Disney+, Hulu, YouTube — are stitched into the video on the server side. DNS blocking can't reliably remove them; it's hit-or-miss depending on the service, device, and blocklist. We won't promise they're gone.

Everything around the stream still gets cleaned up: the tracking, the banners, the telemetry.

Build it yourself

An afternoon and about $70–90 in parts

Everything is open source. Flash an SD card, plug the Pi into your router, run one script, and pair the app. The guide walks you through every step — including the one-time AdGuard wizard and turning on whole-home protection.

No soldering, no Linux experience needed. If you can follow a recipe, you can build this.

Bill of materials

  • Raspberry Pi 5 (2 GB is plenty)
  • microSD card, 16 GB or larger
  • The official Pi 5 power supply (27 W USB-C)
  • One Ethernet cable
  • A phone or computer on the same WiFi
