What's actually happening

Two sentences, no hand-waving

Before any app, TV, or gadget can talk to a server, it asks your network to look up a name — that's a DNS query, and your house makes tens of thousands of them a day.

Holdfast answers those lookups itself: known ad and tracker domains get 0.0.0.0 — an address that goes nowhere — while everything else resolves normally, encrypted on its way out of your house.

The honest ledger

What it kills, and what it can't

Gone

  • Web ads, in every browser on every device
  • In-app banner ads
  • Trackers and telemetry, phones to smart plugs
  • Smart-TV home-screen ads

Still there

  • In-stream video ads on Disney+, Hulu, and YouTube Those ads are stitched into the video server-side. DNS blocking can't reliably remove them — ours included. Sometimes a blocklist catches them, sometimes it doesn't.

Do it yourself

A Raspberry Pi, one script, an afternoon

Flash a card, plug the Pi into your router, run sudo bash deploy/provision.sh. The script installs AdGuard Home and Unbound with encrypted DNS, and the iOS app finds the brick on its own — you just enter a 6-digit pairing code.

Everything is open source. The full guide walks through each step, including the router settings that put every device under protection.
