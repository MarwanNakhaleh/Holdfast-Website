Three things worth knowing

It covers every device.

Phones, TVs, laptops, the tablet in the kitchen, whoever's on your guest WiFi. The brick sits between your network and the internet, so one device protects all of them — no app to put on each one.

Nothing leaves your home.

The blocking happens on a small computer on your own shelf, and its DNS lookups travel encrypted. No cloud account, no company in the middle, no record of your browsing anywhere but your house.

There is no subscription.

You buy the parts once. The software is open source and free, forever. No monthly fee, no plan tiers, no account to cancel.

One thing it can't promise.

Ads inside Disney+, Hulu, and YouTube streams are stitched into the video itself, on their servers. DNS blocking can't reliably remove them — sometimes a blocklist catches them, sometimes they slip through. We won't promise they're gone.

What it does remove: web ads, in-app banners, trackers, telemetry, and smart-TV home-screen ads — the everyday noise, on every screen you own.

Build your own quiet.

A Raspberry Pi, a microSD card, and an afternoon — about $70–90 in parts. The guide walks you through every step, including the fiddly router bits.
